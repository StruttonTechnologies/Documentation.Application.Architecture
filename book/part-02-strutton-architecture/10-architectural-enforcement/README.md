# Chapter 8 — Architectural Enforcement

The previous chapters introduced the architectural responsibilities that make up the Strutton Technologies Application Architecture and explained how each contributes to the overall execution of the system.

Understanding the architecture is only part of the challenge.

The architecture must also be able to preserve itself over time.

Good architectural decisions lose their value if they rely solely on developer discipline. As applications grow and teams evolve, architectural boundaries naturally become blurred unless the structure of the system actively protects them.

This chapter explains how the architecture enforces its own responsibilities, boundaries, and ownership through its physical structure rather than relying on convention alone.

## In this chapter, you will learn

- why architectural enforcement is essential
- how the architecture protects its responsibilities
- how incorrect dependencies are prevented
- why structural enforcement is more effective than convention
- how these principles preserve the architecture over time

By the end of this chapter, you will understand why architectural enforcement is the defining characteristic of the Strutton Technologies Application Architecture and how every architectural decision discussed throughout Part 2 contributes to that goal.
