# Why Architectural Units Matter

Without architectural units, architecture does not hold.

At first, the system may follow the intended structure. Responsibilities are clear, and boundaries are respected. Over time, changes are made, and those rules begin to loosen.

Without enforcement, structure becomes optional.

Developers may introduce dependencies that violate boundaries. Logic may be placed in areas where it does not belong. These decisions are often made for convenience, but they accumulate over time.

This leads to structural decay.

The system becomes harder to understand and harder to maintain. The original architecture may still exist in documentation, but it is no longer reflected in the system itself.

Architectural units prevent this.

They provide a way to enforce structure consistently. They ensure that the rules of the architecture are applied as the system evolves.

This keeps the system aligned with its intended design.
