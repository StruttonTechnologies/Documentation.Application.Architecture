# What Boundaries Are

Architectural boundaries define separation within a system.

While layers group responsibility, boundaries determine where one area of responsibility ends and another begins. They create clear lines that prevent behavior from spreading across the system without control.

A boundary is not a physical barrier.

It is a conceptual constraint that limits how different parts of the system interact. It defines what is allowed to cross from one area to another and what must remain contained.

This distinction is important.

Without boundaries, layers lose their effectiveness. Responsibilities may be grouped, but nothing prevents them from leaking into other areas. Over time, the structure becomes less meaningful because it is no longer enforced.

Boundaries give structure its integrity.

They ensure that responsibilities remain where they belong and that interactions between parts of the system are intentional rather than accidental.
