# Chapter 7 — Architectural Units

In the previous chapters, the structure of a system was defined through layers, boundaries, dependency direction, contracts, and boundary space.

The final step is understanding how that structure is maintained over time.

Architecture is not a one-time design decision. It must be continuously upheld as the system evolves. Without enforcement, even well-designed systems begin to break down.

This chapter introduces the concept of architectural units.

## In this chapter, you will learn

- what architectural units are and how they relate to system structure  
- how architectural units help enforce boundaries and responsibilities  
- why architectural units are necessary for maintaining consistency  
- common ways architectural units break down in real systems  

This is not about specific implementations. It is about understanding how architectural structure is preserved as systems grow and change.
